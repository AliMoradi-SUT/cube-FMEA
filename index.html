PRIORITY 01

Loss of first contact / no reliable TT&C

Critical
Preliminary combined RPN: 5 × 4 × 4 = 80Screening score — not a measured probability

Historical evidence

UNSW-EC0 and INSPIRE-2 initially appeared lost and were recovered with external ground-station support; E-ST@R-II had a serious downlink/link-margin problem; CuPID's radio system was identified as the most probable cause of prolonged no-response. Root causes and certainty differ by case.

Related project tasks

U/V/S transceiver evaluation; link budget; TTC board; antenna deployment/design; radiation-pattern simulation; command/telemetry dictionary and GDS planning.

Possible on-orbit symptoms

No beacon, weak/intermittent signal, beacon-only operation, uplink commands not acknowledged, or contact that changes with attitude/ground station.

Recommended engineering response

Prioritize a conservative end-to-end link budget with margin; verify antenna deployment and feedline; perform integrated radio–antenna–flight-software–ground-station testing; define a low-rate, low-power beacon and a small set of recovery commands; rehearse orbit/object identification and contact windows with more than one station if available.

Low-cost educational mission decision: VERY HIGH — mission-enabling. Spend effort on integration, test time, antenna geometry and ground procedures before paying for a higher-spec radio. Use accessible amateur/university stations where legally permitted; do not assume a more powerful transmitter fixes a bad antenna or ground procedure.

Evidence links: UNSW recovery report · E-ST@R-II investigation · NASA CuPID lessons

PRIORITY 02

Power deficit, battery undervoltage or EPS reset

Critical
Preliminary combined RPN: 5 × 4 × 4 = 80Screening score — not a measured probability

Historical evidence

IDEASSat/INSPIRESat-2's investigation identified an EPS reset-circuit / single-event-latch-up issue; INSPIRE-2's recovery involved allowing the battery to charge before another antenna deployment attempt. Historical cases show that a power issue can look like a radio or deployment failure.

Related project tasks

SSO 500 km solar estimate; 28 Wh battery selection; MPPT/regulators; 3.3 V/5 V buses; battery protection/monitoring; thermal & power analysis.

Possible on-orbit symptoms

Repeated resets, declining battery voltage, no contact after eclipse, radio dropout during transmit, unexpected protection trips, or recovery only after charging.

Recommended engineering response

Calculate worst-case orbit energy balance including eclipse, seasonal beta angle, pointing, array degradation, temperature and conversion losses; verify usable battery energy at mission conditions; log battery voltage/current/temperature and reset cause; test radio-transmit peak load and safe low-power recovery.

Low-cost educational mission decision: VERY HIGH — mission-enabling. A conservative power budget, load shedding and software duty-cycle limits are usually cheaper than upgrading the battery late. Avoid deploying antennas or running payloads repeatedly when battery state is low.

Evidence links: IDEASSat lessons learned · NASA power subsystem · INSPIRE-2 recovery report

Practical budget allocation

Protect first

Power-positive survival, beacon/command recovery, MCU reset containment, boot rollback, persistent health telemetry and a reliable antenna/RF path.

Verify cheaply

Use hardware-in-the-loop tests, simulated battery/solar inputs, interrupted-update tests, RF conducted/over-the-air tests, command rehearsals, sensor/actuator fault injection and checklists.

Accept consciously

Non-rad-tolerant MCU residual risk; no fourth reaction wheel; limited hardware redundancy; modest payload cadence/data rate. Each acceptance needs a named owner, rationale, triggers and recovery plan.

Do not economize blindly

Do not remove battery protection, basic power margin, watchdog/reset recovery, antenna integration tests or the ability to diagnose a silent spacecraft. A cheaper part can be reasonable; an untested critical interface is not.

Evidence limitations

  • The risk ordering should be revisited when exact MCU/radio/battery/wheel part numbers, mission duration, orbit radiation estimate, ground station and power budget are known.

Preliminary design completion gates — checks before moving to the next step

This section revisits every task in the current design plan and defines what the team should check, analyze, document and review before marking that task complete. It is deliberately scoped to conceptual and preliminary design for an educational, low-cost 1.5U CubeSat.

What “complete” means at this stage: requirements and assumptions are documented; a feasible concept is selected; first-order calculations close with stated margins; interfaces are identified; high risks have owners and mitigations; and the next-phase verification method is planned. It does not mean flight hardware is qualified or that analysis has replaced later integration and environmental testing.
Three project-specific controls: (1) the selected MCU is not radiation-tolerant and its residual risk must remain visible; (2) three reaction wheels are selected, so three-wheel controllability and single-wheel-out behavior must be assessed without assuming a fourth wheel; (3) the “pyramid mount” task must be reconciled with the actual three-wheel geometry before the mount concept is frozen.

Recommended gate-review workflow

  1. Owner self-check: task owner completes the checks and attaches calculations, datasheets, diagrams or trade study.
  2. Interface review: a teammate from the neighboring subsystem checks shared assumptions, power/data/mechanical interfaces and failure dependencies.
  3. Risk update: update the combined risk register with new evidence, residual risk and any change to likelihood/detectability assumptions.

Cross-subsystem preliminary design review (PDR) exit checklist

  • Mission objectives, orbit assumptions, design life and success criteria are stated and consistent across subsystems.
  • Mass, volume, power/energy, data storage/downlink and thermal budgets have explicit margins and named assumptions.
  • Every critical interface has an owner and a preliminary definition: electrical, data, RF, mechanical, thermal and operational.
  • TT&C and low-power recovery are treated as mission-enabling; commissioning and no-contact troubleshooting procedures exist at outline level.
  • MCU radiation limitation, three-wheel ADCS limitations and any other accepted compromises are recorded as residual risks with decision owners.
  • High-priority risks have mitigations that are feasible for the educational budget and a verification method planned for the next phase.
  • No unresolved contradiction remains between task names, selected parts, CAD layout, interface definitions and subsystem assumptions.
  • The team has a clear next-phase plan: detailed design, prototype/flat-sat integration, verification and later environmental/launch-provider requirements.

These gates are tailored engineering guidance, not a claim that any task has already passed. Record evidence and reviewer decisions before changing task status. Final acceptance criteria must be adjusted to actual component datasheets, orbit, mission duration, launch-provider constraints and institutional requirements.

Project-wide actions

  1. Record the MCU radiation-risk decision: part number, available radiation data, orbit/lifetime assumptions, mitigations, verification evidence and residual-risk owner. Software mitigation does not make a non-rad-tolerant MCU radiation-tolerant.
  2. Demonstrate recovery: test watchdog reset, safe-mode entry, dual-bank rollback, corrupted-image rejection and persistent fault telemetry.
  3. Close power/TT&C interfaces early: verify radio transmit current cannot reset the MCU, payload faults cannot collapse critical rails, and the flight radio/antenna/ground station work as one chain.
  4. Resolve the three-wheel geometry: confirm axes and controllability, simulate each single-wheel-out case, and determine whether magnetorquers support safe degraded recovery.
  5. Link mitigations to evidence: assign a test or analysis artifact and acceptance criterion to each risk. A mitigation is not closed simply because it is written in a plan.
  6. Re-score at milestones: after part selection, schematic review, PCB bring-up, integrated flat-sat, vibration/thermal testing and mission readiness review. Track residual risk separately.
